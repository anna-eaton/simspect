How the checker decides a violation, on a real gem5 trace (InvisiSpec build, test inst-002014). Each bar is one instruction from fetch (F) through decode (D) and issue (E) to retirement; dotted stretches are elided cycles. The roles lc (last committed), fnc (first non-committed, here the unresolved branch) and xmit (transmitter) come from labels in the Alloy instance, and the unresolved window runs from lc's retirement to the branch's resolution. Inside that window the checker applies the defense's transmission criterion (shaded): xmit's L1D eviction ($\star$) meets InvisiSpec's criterion before xmit is squashed, so the test is reported as a violation.
