\textbf{Recon-STT lets a tainted load complete through store-to-load forwarding} (gem5-recon-modded, STT scheme, test inst-014912). Top: the test's execution graph, in the notation of Table~\ref{tab:execvocab}; i2 is the unresolved branch, so i3--i5 are speculative; i3 loads the protected $m0$ into $r0$ (\textit{ddi}), and $r0$ is the address of both the store i4 and the transmitter i5 (\textit{rf} into i5, thick: the leak path). Bottom: the two instructions in the gem5 trace (cycle = (tick $-$ 2679000)/500). At cycle 5 Recon's own check reports i5 tainted and speculative, yet i5 takes i4's value from the store queue and completes at cycle 7 ($\star$), before the squash at 9. STT requires a tainted-address load, and the forwarding decision itself, to wait until the address is untainted (STT, Sec.~6.4.2, 6.5). In Recon the taint check sits only on the cache path, after the forwarding path has returned (blue).
